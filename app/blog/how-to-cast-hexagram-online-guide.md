# How to Cast Hexagrams Online — Step by Step Guide

Casting a hexagram is the heart of I Ching divination. It is the moment when your focused intention meets the ancient system of 64 hexagrams — creating a bridge between your specific question and the timeless patterns of the *Book of Changes*.

Whether you prefer the tactile ritual of tossing coins, the meditative complexity of yarrow stalks, or the convenience of a digital tool, the process of casting a hexagram follows the same underlying logic. This guide walks you through **every method**, from traditional to digital, so you can cast hexagrams with confidence and clarity.

## The Basic Principle: Six Lines, One Hexagram

Every hexagram in the I Ching consists of **six lines**, drawn from bottom to top. Each line is either:
- **Solid (—)** — representing Yang, the active, creative force
- **Broken (- -)** — representing Yin, the receptive, nurturing force

But there's a subtlety: in traditional casting, each line can also be **changing** (old yin or old yang). A changing line is one that is in a state of transformation — it will flip to its opposite. This creates a **primary hexagram** (your current situation) and a **transformed hexagram** (where things are heading).

The four possible line outcomes from a single toss are:

| Value | Name | Type | Behavior |
|-------|------|------|----------|
| 6 | Old Yin | Broken | Changing → becomes solid |
| 7 | Young Yang | Solid | Stable → stays solid |
| 8 | Young Yin | Broken | Stable → stays broken |
| 9 | Old Yang | Solid | Changing → becomes broken |

You need **six tosses** to build a complete hexagram, each toss determining one line from bottom to top.

## Method 1: The Traditional Coin Method

The coin method is the most accessible traditional technique. You need only **three coins** and a quiet moment.

### What You Need
- Three identical coins (any denomination works; Chinese cash coins are traditional but not required)
- A quiet, comfortable space
- A surface to toss the coins onto
- Something to record your results (paper and pen)

### Step-by-Step Instructions

**Step 1: Prepare Your Space**

Find a quiet place where you won't be disturbed for about 10–15 minutes. Sit comfortably. Some practitioners light a candle or incense to mark the ritual space, but this is optional.

**Step 2: Formulate Your Question**

Hold your question clearly in your mind. It should be open-ended and meaningful. Instead of "Will I get the job?" try "What energy surrounds my job application, and what approach should I take?"

**Step 3: Set the Coin Faces**

Decide which side of your coin represents yin and which represents yang. A common convention:
- **Heads (image side)** = 3 points = Yang
- **Tails (number side)** = 2 points = Yin

You'll toss all three coins together and add up the points.

**Step 4: Toss the Coins — First Time**

Hold all three coins in your cupped hands. Shake them gently while holding your question in mind. Release them onto your surface.

Count the points:
- **6 points** (all tails: 2+2+2) = Old Yin (changing broken line)
- **7 points** (two tails, one head: 2+2+3) = Young Yang (stable solid line)
- **8 points** (one tail, two heads: 2+3+3) = Young Yin (stable broken line)
- **9 points** (all heads: 3+3+3) = Old Yang (changing solid line)

Record the result. This is **Line 1** (the bottom line).

**Step 5: Toss Five More Times**

Repeat the tossing process five more times, recording each result. Each toss creates the next line upward:

| Toss | Line Position | Result |
|------|--------------|--------|
| 1st toss | Line 1 (bottom) | Record |
| 2nd toss | Line 2 | Record |
| 3rd toss | Line 3 | Record |
| 4th toss | Line 4 | Record |
| 5th toss | Line 5 | Record |
| 6th toss | Line 6 (top) | Record |

**Step 6: Draw Your Hexagram**

Now draw your six lines from bottom to top. For example, if your results were:

| Line | Points | Type | Symbol |
|------|--------|------|--------|
| 6 (top) | 8 | Young Yin | - - |
| 5 | 7 | Young Yang | — |
| 4 | 9 | Old Yang | —○ |
| 3 | 7 | Young Yang | — |
| 2 | 6 | Old Yin | - -○ |
| 1 (bottom) | 7 | Young Yang | — |

The circles (○) indicate changing lines. Your hexagram has two changing lines (lines 2 and 4).

**Step 7: Identify the Hexagrams**

Look up your hexagram in a reference table or use an online tool. The primary hexagram is formed by your original lines. If you have changing lines, the transformed hexagram is found by flipping those lines (old yin becomes solid, old yang becomes broken).

**Step 8: Read the Interpretation**

Read the I Ching text for your primary hexagram, paying special attention to the **changing lines**. The changing lines often contain the most specific guidance for your question. Then read the transformed hexagram to understand the likely direction of events.

## Method 2: The Yarrow Stalk Method

The yarrow stalk method is the **oldest recorded method** of I Ching casting, described in the *Xici Zhuan* (Great Commentary) attributed to Confucius himself. It is more complex and time-consuming than the coin method but is considered by many practitioners to be the most authentic.

### What You Need
- 50 yarrow stalks (or wooden sticks, toothpicks, or similar thin rods)
- A quiet space
- Time (this method takes 20–30 minutes)
- Patience

### Step-by-Step Instructions

The yarrow stalk method is more involved. Each of the six lines requires a multi-step process:

**Step 1: Set Aside One Stalk**

Take all 50 stalks. Remove one and set it aside. This represents the **Taiji** (太极, Supreme Ultimate) — the undivided source from which all things emerge. You now work with 49 stalks.

**Step 2: First Division**

Randomly divide the 49 stalks into two piles, holding one in each hand. This represents the division of heaven and earth.

Remove one stalk from the right pile and hold it between your little finger and ring finger. This represents **humanity** — the space between heaven and earth.

**Step 3: Second Division**

Take the left pile and remove stalks one at a time, grouping them into sets of four. Hold any remainder (1, 2, 3, or 4 stalks) between your ring and middle fingers.

Take the right pile and similarly group into sets of four. Hold the remainder between your middle and index fingers.

The remainders from both piles will always total **4 or 8**.

**Step 4: Third Division**

Set aside the stalks you've processed (the single stalk + both remainders). You now have 45 or 41 stalks remaining.

Repeat the entire division process (Steps 2–3) two more times with the remaining stalks.

After three divisions, the remaining stalks will total either **24, 28, 32, or 36**.

**Step 5: Determine the Line Value**

Divide the remaining count by 4:
- **24 ÷ 4 = 6** → Old Yin (changing broken)
- **28 ÷ 4 = 7** → Young Yang (stable solid)
- **32 ÷ 4 = 8** → Young Yin (stable broken)
- **36 ÷ 4 = 9** → Old Yang (changing solid)

Record the line. Repeat the entire process (Steps 1–5) five more times to generate all six lines.

> **Note:** The yarrow stalk method produces a slightly different probability distribution than the coin method. Each outcome has a probability of approximately 1/4, but the exact distribution differs. This is one reason traditionalists consider the yarrow stalk method more "authentic."

## Method 3: Digital / Online Hexagram Casting

Modern technology has made hexagram casting accessible to anyone with a smartphone or computer. Digital tools simulate the traditional methods using random number generation.

### How Digital Casting Works

Online hexagram casting tools work by generating random numbers that map to the four line outcomes (6, 7, 8, 9). Behind the scenes:

1. The tool uses a **pseudorandom number generator** (PRNG)
2. Each "toss" produces a value between 6 and 9
3. Six values are generated sequentially, building the hexagram from bottom to top
4. The tool identifies the hexagram and calculates any transformed hexagram

### Step-by-Step: Using an Online Tool

**Step 1: Choose a Reputable Tool**

Look for a tool that provides:
- Clear hexagram identification
- Full I Ching text for each hexagram and line
- The transformed hexagram (if applicable)
- Explanatory commentary

You can use the [FateWise Bagua tool](/bagua) for a related Chinese metaphysics experience, or search for dedicated I Ching divination websites.

**Step 2: Focus on Your Question**

Before clicking any button, take three deep breaths. Hold your question clearly in your awareness. Many online tools include a prompt asking you to type your question — use this as a focusing device.

**Step 3: Cast the Hexagram**

Click the cast/generate button. Some tools simulate the coin toss with an animation; others simply generate the result. The method doesn't matter — what matters is your **intention**.

**Step 4: Review the Result**

The tool will display:
- The primary hexagram number and name
- The line values (6, 7, 8, 9)
- Any changing lines
- The transformed hexagram (if applicable)
- The classical text for each relevant hexagram and line

**Step 5: Read and Reflect**

Read through the full interpretation. Don't rush. The I Ching's wisdom often reveals itself through repeated reading and reflection.

### Advantages of Digital Casting

- **Instant access** — No materials needed
- **Full text included** — No need to look up interpretations separately
- **Record keeping** — Many tools save your reading history
- **Educational** — Built-in explanations help you learn the system
- **Accessible** — Works on any device, anywhere

### Disadvantages of Digital Casting

- **Less ritual** — The physical process is part of the meditation
- **Algorithmic randomness** — Some practitioners prefer physical randomness
- **Less embodied** — The tactile experience of coins or stalks creates a different mental state

## Method 4: The Digital Coin Simulation

Some online tools offer a more immersive experience by simulating the coin-tossing process visually. These tools often include:

- Animated coin tosses
- Visual display of each line being built
- Sound effects for the toss
- Step-by-step progression through all six lines

This hybrid approach combines the **ritual feel** of the traditional method with the **convenience** of digital interpretation.

### How to Use a Digital Coin Tool

1. Open the tool and focus on your question
2. Click "Toss Coins" — the tool animates three coins
3. Observe the result and note the line type
4. Repeat six times, building the hexagram from bottom to top
5. Review the full interpretation when all six lines are complete

## Interpreting Your Cast Hexagram

Once you've cast your hexagram by any method, here's how to read the result:

### Understanding the Primary Hexagram

The primary hexagram describes your **current situation**. Read the hexagram name, the judgment (overall meaning), and the image (symbolic representation). This gives you the broad context.

### Reading the Changing Lines

If your hexagram has changing lines, these are the **most important parts** of the reading. Each changing line has its own text that provides specific guidance. Read them in order from line 1 to line 6.

**Example:** If lines 2 and 5 are changing, read the texts for both lines. They often address different aspects of your situation.

### Understanding the Transformed Hexagram

The transformed hexagram shows where the situation is **heading**. If many lines are changing (more than 3), some practitioners consider the transformed hexagram to be the primary reading and the original to be background context.

### Synthesizing the Reading

The most insightful readings come from **synthesizing** all three elements:
1. What the primary hexagram says about your current situation
2. What the changing lines say about what's shifting
3. What the transformed hexagram says about the likely direction

## Practical Tips for Hexagram Casting

### Create a Ritual Space

Whether physical or digital, establish a consistent ritual:
- Same time of day if possible
- Same quiet space
- Three deep breaths before casting
- Clear formulation of the question

### Keep a Reading Journal

Record every reading:
- Date and time
- Your question
- The hexagram(s) received
- Your interpretation at the time
- What actually happened (follow up weeks or months later)

This journal becomes an invaluable tool for developing your intuition and understanding how the I Ching speaks to your specific life patterns.

### Don't Cast for Trivial Questions

Save the hexagram for questions that genuinely matter. The I Ching rewards depth of inquiry with depth of response.

### Trust Your First Impression

When you first read the hexagram text, notice your immediate emotional reaction. Does it feel relevant? Surprising? Comforting? Your first impression often carries important intuitive information.

## Frequently Asked Questions

### How many times can I cast for the same question?

Traditionally, **once per question**. If the reading is unclear, you may rephrase the question and cast again. But repeatedly casting the same question to get a different answer is considered improper.

### What if I get a hexagram with no changing lines?

This is common — about 1 in 16 casts produces no changing lines. In this case, read only the primary hexagram's judgment and image. The situation is stable, and the guidance is in the overall hexagram meaning.

### Can I cast a hexagram for someone else?

Yes, but only with that person's knowledge and consent. The I Ching responds to genuine inquiry, and casting for someone without their awareness is considered disrespectful to the oracle.

### How long does it take to become proficient at casting?

The mechanical process of casting takes minutes to learn. Developing the **intuition** to interpret readings meaningfully takes months or years of practice and reflection.
