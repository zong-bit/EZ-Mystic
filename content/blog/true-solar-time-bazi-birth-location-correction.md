# True Solar Time in Bazi: Why Your Birth Location Matters

Did you know that your Bazi chart might be wrong?

Not because Bazi is inaccurate — but because most free Bazi calculators don't apply **True Solar Time correction**.

Here's the issue: your birth certificate time is based on your **time zone's standard time**, not the actual position of the sun above your birthplace. But Bazi was developed over 2,000 years ago, when time was measured by a sundial — not a clock.

If your birthplace is far from your time zone's standard meridian, your solar time could be **30 minutes or more** different from your clock time. And in Bazi, 30 minutes can change your **Hour Pillar** — which affects everything from your career to your children.

## What is True Solar Time?

True Solar Time (真太阳时) is the actual time based on the sun's position at your exact location. It's what a sundial would read if you placed it at your birthplace.

Modern clock time divides the Earth into 24 time zones, each one hour apart. But the sun doesn't follow time zones — it reaches its zenith at slightly different times depending on your longitude.

## How to Calculate True Solar Time

The formula is simple:

```
True Solar Time = Standard Time + (Longitude Correction) + (Equation of Time)
```

### Step 1: Longitude Correction

For every degree of longitude you're from your time zone's standard meridian, add or subtract 4 minutes:

- **East of standard meridian**: Add 4 minutes per degree
- **West of standard meridian**: Subtract 4 minutes per degree

Standard meridians by time zone:
| Time Zone | Standard Meridian |
|-----------|------------------|
| UTC-8 (PST) | 120°W |
| UTC-5 (EST) | 75°W |
| UTC+0 (GMT) | 0° |
| UTC+1 (CET) | 15°E |
| UTC+8 (CST China) | 120°E |

**Example**: Someone born at 2:00 PM in Los Angeles (118°W):
- LA is 2° east of the 120°W standard meridian
- Correction: +8 minutes
- Solar time: 2:08 PM (before equation of time)

### Step 2: Equation of Time

The Earth's orbit is elliptical, not circular, and its axis is tilted. This means the length of a solar day varies throughout the year — by up to 16 minutes.

The Equation of Time correction varies by date:

| Month | Approximate Correction |
|-------|----------------------|
| Jan | -5 to -10 minutes |
| Feb-Mar | -5 to +5 minutes |
| Apr-May | 0 to +5 minutes |
| Jun | 0 minutes |
| Jul-Aug | -5 to +5 minutes |
| Sep-Nov | +5 to +15 minutes |
| Dec | -5 minutes |

## Examples

### Example 1: China (Same Time Zone, Different Longitude)

China uses a single time zone (UTC+8, based on 120°E), despite spanning 62° of longitude. A person born at:

- **Beijing (116°E)**: 4° west of standard meridian → -16 minutes correction
- **Urumqi (87°E)**: 33° west of standard meridian → -132 minutes correction

Someone born at 1:00 PM clock time in Urumqi has a **True Solar Time of about 10:48 AM** — a completely different Hour Pillar (changed from Wei to Si).

### Example 2: United States

Someone born at 8:30 AM EST in:
- **New York (74°W)**: 1° east of standard meridian → +4 minutes → 8:34 AM solar
- **Boston (71°W)**: 4° east of standard meridian → +16 minutes → 8:46 AM solar

Still within the same Hour Pillar (Chen, 7-9 AM), but close to the boundary.

### Example 3: Near a Branch Boundary

Someone born at 10:50 PM in London (0° longitude) in December:
- Longitude correction: zero (on standard meridian)
- Equation of time (Dec): -5 minutes
- True Solar Time: 10:45 PM

Still Hai branch (9-11 PM), but if they were born at 11:05 PM clock time:
- True Solar Time: 11:00 PM
- This is the Zi branch boundary — a single minute can determine whether they're Hai or Zi.
